# Video scene patterns

Choose one primary pattern. Combine patterns only when the duration and live mode can support a coherent result.

## Cinematic continuous shot

- Define opening composition, subject action, camera path, environmental motion, and ending beat.
- Use one primary camera move at a time.
- Anchor scale and spatial orientation so motion is easy to follow.
- Best for mood, character moments, product reveals, transformations, and image animation.

## Image-to-video

- Treat the source as locked composition and identity, not a broad mood reference.
- Describe plausible movement: breathing, cloth or hair movement, gaze, hand action, environmental effects, and camera drift.
- State what must remain stable: face, body proportions, product shape, logo, architecture, and color.
- Avoid combining large subject movement, aggressive camera movement, and a major scene transformation in a short clip.

## Product showcase

- **Studio reveal:** Controlled orbit or push-in, designed reflection changes, clean surfaces, and a clear silhouette.
- **Feature demo:** One feature or action per shot, clear hand interaction, plausible mechanics, and no invented capability.
- **Lifestyle:** Show context and benefit through action without unsupported claims.
- **Unboxing:** Establish sealed product, opening, reveal, and reaction or detail. Use multiple shots only when requested.
- **Commercial:** Sequence hook, product evidence, and hero close-up. Leave slogans and end cards for deterministic post-production unless supplied by the user.

## UGC and presenter video

Use natural eye-level framing, believable phone movement, everyday settings, and imperfect but intentional performance. If live tools do not accept synchronized audio, plan speech separately. Never invent testimonials, personal experience, before-and-after effects, endorsements, or prices.

## Explainers

Use one point per visual section: problem, mechanism, demonstration, and user-provided call to action. Match visual complexity to narration duration. Prefer simple representative scenes, diagrams, object metaphors, or controlled product demonstrations over unreadable on-screen paragraphs.

## Multi-shot narrative

Use multiple shots only when requested or when place, time, scale, or information state changes. Give each shot a purpose, duration, framing, action, and continuity anchor. Preserve wardrobe, face, product structure, light direction, color script, screen direction, and location details.

## Social formats

- **9:16:** Show the visual hook immediately, keep action centered vertically, and reserve the lower UI-safe area.
- **1:1 or 4:5:** Use medium framing, clear silhouettes, and restrained lateral movement.
- **16:9:** Use wider spatial orientation, stronger entrances or reveals, and intentional negative space.

## Concept options

When the user asks for ideas, offer three different directions: demonstration, emotion, and spectacle. For each, state the hook, shot structure, camera behavior, duration fit, and protected brand or reference facts.
